---
title: "When we had a place on the internet"
date: 2026-09-23
description: "GeoCities, Neocities, and the people bringing back the handcrafted web."
---

If you were around in the early days of the internet, you probably remember personal websites: handcrafted little corners of the web where someone shared their hobbies, interests, and thoughts. They weren't exactly great in terms of UX or UI. Pages were crowded, full of bright blue links, and way too many of them had that dreadful "Under Construction" GIF that never went away. But you could tell a real person was behind them.

Back then, the internet was about permanence and identity: putting your mark somewhere and showing the world you existed. Now most of us just have a profile on someone else's platform, and they all look the same. We also used to find things differently. There was no feed deciding what you saw. You went looking for what you wanted, and that intentionality is mostly gone.

Many of those sites lived on GeoCities. It shut down in 2009, but the idea never really died. Today, some people are bringing that spirit back, and one of the main places they're doing it is [Neocities](https://neocities.org/).

Browsing Neocities feels like the old web. Sites are built by hand, with intention, and they look like whoever made them. There's also a sense of community. Instead of an algorithm picking what you see, everyone links to each other's sites with tiny buttons and animated blinkies. Finding something new just means following a link someone else left for you.
