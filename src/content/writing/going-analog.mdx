---
title: "Going Analog"
date: 2026-02-10
description: "When everything competes for attention."
---

As someone who grew up in the 90s, I find something very special about technology from that era.

I remember walking to the video rental store on Fridays to pick a VHS for the weekend, rushing home to play my favorite PlayStation game on a CRT, and taking my Game Boy to school to trade Pokémon with friends.

I remember listening to a mixtape on a Walkman that I had carefully curated from other tapes, and wearing a Casio F91-W that felt incredibly cool simply because it had an alarm, a stopwatch, and that green glowing backlight.

It could just be nostalgia, but I think these things felt special because there were fewer distractions, fewer conveniences, and fewer alternatives competing for attention.

Everything now comes with a notification, a feed, or a next episode. The same pocket device handles your music, your messages, your news, and your camera. It's efficient, but that efficiency has a cost.

That's one of the reasons why, in recent years, I've found myself gravitating back toward these older technologies.

I've recently picked up a CRT to plug my old consoles. Got into collecting Game Boy carts and accessories. Listening to a record on vinyl or cassette from start to finish. And replacing a smart watch with vintage Casio digital watches.

Not because they're better. But because they encourage a focused, distraction-free experience.

It's a deliberate choice to slow down and embrace a little inefficiency. There's something clarifying about a device that only does one thing, and does it without asking for anything back.
