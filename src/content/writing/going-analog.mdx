---
title: "Going Analog"
date: 2026-02-10
description: "When everything competes for attention."
---

As someone who grew up in the 90s, I find something very special about technology from that era.

I remember trading Pokémon on my Game Boy at school, rushing home to play PlayStation on a CRT, and walking to the video rental store on Fridays to pick a VHS for the weekend.

I also remember listening to my favorite tapes on a Walkman, and wearing a [Casio watch that could change the TV channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4RypymzRge4) that felt like the peak of technology.

It could just be nostalgia, but I think these things felt special because there were fewer distractions, fewer conveniences, and fewer alternatives competing for attention.

Modern life, by contrast, fragments our focus through multitasking, algorithms, and efficiency-driven habits. That's one of the reasons why, in recent years, I've been going back more and more toward these older technologies.

These technologies encouraged a focused, distraction-free experience rather than something optimized or multitasked.

It's a deliberate choice to slow down, embrace a bit of inefficiency, and rediscover the joy that comes from being fully present in a hobby.
