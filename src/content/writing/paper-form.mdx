Everything Linear does, they do with so much taste.

I especially enjoy their thing for retro looks. They usually play with diagrams that look like a terminal or markdown, or with dithering, and this time they went with a whole site that looks printed on paper: [Orbit](https://orbit.linear.app/), the site for their upcoming conference.

So I had to try it on a little form.

<PaperForm />

The look comes from three textures, but the interesting part is how they're used.

## Grain as a mask

The grain isn't laid on top of the text. It's used as a mask, so the speckles knock tiny holes in the ink, as if it didn't fully transfer to the paper. Every piece of "ink" gets it: titles, labels, inputs, even the underlines of the fields. A half-pixel text shadow in the text's own color adds a bit of ink bleed.

```css title="paper-form.module.css"
.ink {
  text-shadow: 0 0 0.5px currentColor;
  mask: url(/textures/grain.webp) 0 0 / 128px 128px;
}
```

The button does the opposite: a white layer masked to the _inverse_ of the grain, so the solid block gets white flecks where the ink didn't take.

## A paper tile over everything

Then a paper texture is tiled over the whole sheet with `mix-blend-mode: darken`. Because it sits on top, it textures the ink as well as the paper, and that's what makes it read as printed instead of just placed on a beige background.

```css title="paper-form.module.css"
.sheet::after {
  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  pointer-events: none;
  background: url(/textures/paper-tile.avif) 0 0 / 158px 158px;
  mix-blend-mode: darken;
  opacity: 0.3;
}
```

## Fingerprints

Finally, the details. A scanned fingerprint with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` at very low opacity, sitting in a corner like someone handled the sheet. You barely notice it, which is the point.

The textures in this demo are Linear's, taken from the Orbit site, so all the credit goes to them.
